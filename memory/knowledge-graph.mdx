Individual memories tell agents isolated facts. The knowledge graph connects those facts into a structured picture of the world — linking people, companies, projects, and concepts into a web of relationships that agents can reason about.

## What the knowledge graph is

As agents work, they don't just store facts — they identify entities (a person, a company, a project) and the relationships between them. These connections form a graph:

```
Sarah Chen → works at → Meridian Corp
Meridian Corp → is client of → Project Falcon
Project Falcon → has deadline → August 2025
Project Falcon → depends on → API integration
```

When you ask "what do we have coming up with Meridian?", the agent doesn't just search for "Meridian" — it traverses the graph and surfaces everything connected: the contact, the project, the deadline, and the dependencies.

This is faster and more reliable than searching flat memory for complex relational questions.

## How it's built

The knowledge graph is built automatically as agents work. You don't configure or populate it manually.

When an agent stores a memory, it also:

1. Identifies entities mentioned (people, companies, products, locations, dates)
2. Identifies the relationships between those entities
3. Creates or updates nodes and edges in the graph

Over time, the graph grows to reflect your actual business context — clients, projects, team members, tools, and how they all connect.

## Viewing the knowledge graph

Go to **Dashboard → Knowledge Graph** to see an interactive visualisation of your entity graph.

- **Nodes** represent entities (people, companies, projects, etc.)
- **Edges** represent relationships between them
- Click any node to see its associated memories and connections
- Use the search box to jump to a specific entity
- Filter by entity type (Person, Company, Project, etc.)

The graph updates in real time as agents store new memories.

## Why it matters for agents

Without a knowledge graph, agents answer relational questions by searching for each entity separately and hoping the results connect. With a knowledge graph, they can traverse relationships directly.

Practical difference:

| Question | Without graph | With graph |
|---|---|---|
| "Who's my contact at Meridian?" | Searches for "Meridian contact" — may miss it | Traverses: Meridian Corp → primary contact → Sarah Chen |
| "What projects are at risk this quarter?" | Searches for "at risk" memories | Traverses: all projects → with deadline in Q3 → flagged as blocked |
| "Has Apex Corp worked with us before?" | May not find an older memory | Checks the entity graph for any historical Apex Corp connections |

## Entities Qorven tracks automatically

- **People** — names, roles, email addresses, organizations
- **Companies** — clients, vendors, partners
- **Projects** — names, goals, deadlines, team members
- **Products** — things you sell or build
- **Locations** — offices, regions, delivery addresses
- **Dates and deadlines** — linked to the projects and people they belong to

<Tip>
  The graph is most useful after a few weeks of regular use. The more your agents work — researching, communicating, tracking tasks — the richer and more accurate the graph becomes.
</Tip>
